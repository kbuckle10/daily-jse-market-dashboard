How the scorecard is derived

Existing data • 0–100 lenses

Each factor score uses the financial, valuation, dividend and price-trend data already collected. Available inputs are scored against sector-aware ranges and averaged within each lens; unavailable fields are ignored rather than treated as zero.

V
ValuationIs the stock cheap or stretched?
P/EP/BFair value upsideBuy-zone position

Lower sensible earnings/book multiples and stronger upside to fair value score better. P/B receives greater relevance for banks, insurers and investment companies.

Q
QualityHow efficiently does the business earn?
ROEROA

Higher sustainable returns on equity and assets indicate stronger business economics and receive higher scores.

G
GrowthAre earnings and sales expanding?
EPS growthRevenue growthNet-income growth

Positive, durable earnings and revenue growth score best; contractions reduce the growth lens.

F
Financial strengthCan the balance sheet support the thesis?
Debt / EquityCurrent ratioROA

Lower leverage, adequate liquidity and productive assets improve the score. Liquidity ratios are de-emphasized for financial institutions.

D
DividendIs the income attractive and sustainable?
Trailing yieldPayout ratioDividend growthPayment recency

Attractive yield helps only when supported by a reasonable payout, stable or growing dividends, and a recent payment. Stale dividend history is flagged and should not be treated as current income.

M
MomentumWhat is the market trend?
1M3M6M1Y

Broader positive price trends score higher, while sharp deterioration lowers the timing/momentum lens.

Important: the six lenses explain the ranking context; they do not replace the primary Buy / Hold / Avoid rating, valuation signal or target buy zone.
